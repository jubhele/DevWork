## PART ONE: THE CERTIFICATION PATH

Getting certified in South Africa is expensive, slow, and bureaucratic by design. This section maps every step, every cost, and every shortcut.

---

### Step 1 — Register Your Drone (Week 1)

Before anything else, every drone over 250g must be registered with the SACAA.

**What you need:**
- Drone make, model, and serial number
- Intended use (commercial)
- Operator contact information

**How:** Visit caa.co.za → RPAS Registration  
**Cost:** Nominal registration fee (< R500)  
**Output:** Unique registration number, must be physically displayed on the aircraft

> Do this the day the drone arrives. An unregistered commercial drone is an uninsured liability.

---

### Step 2 — Train Your First Pilot: The RPC Course (Weeks 2–10)

The Remote Pilot Certificate (RPC) is the individual licence every commercial drone pilot must hold. No RPC, no legal flight.

**Course requirements:**
- Theory component (can be completed via Zoom at some providers)
- Practical flying component (in-person, Midrand or Cape Town typically)
- SACAA written exam
- Class 3 aviation medical from a SACAA-approved Aviation Medical Examiner
- Practical flying demonstration

**Cost per pilot:**  
- RPC course: R25,000–R50,000 (varies by provider)  
- Medical certificate: R2,500–R5,000  
- Total per pilot: R27,500–R55,000

**Timeline:** 6–8 weeks including theory, practicals, exam submission, and SACAA processing

**Approved Training Organisations (ATOs) to approach:**
- NTSU Aviation (ntsuaviation.com)
- RocketDNA (rocketdna.com)
- DC Geomatics (dcgeomatics.co.za) — KZN-based, ROC partner potential

> Train two pilots minimum. One absence should never ground operations.

---

### Step 3 — Night Operations Authorization (Parallel, Weeks 6–12)

AECI Chempark security is a 24/7 requirement. Standard SACAA rules prohibit night flight without specific authorization.

**What to apply for:** Night operations endorsement on your ROC  
**When to start:** Submit the application in parallel with your ROC application — do not wait  
**Additional requirements:** Documented procedures, lighting systems on aircraft, enhanced risk assessment

> This is non-negotiable for a security contract. Confirm night ops approval before committing a delivery date to AECI.

---

### Step 4 — Apply for the ROC (Months 1–12)

The Remote Operator Certificate is the company-level certification. Without it, no commercial drone operation is legal in South Africa, regardless of individual pilot licences.

**What SACAA requires:**

| Post Holder | Role |
|---|---|
| Accountable Manager | Overall organisational responsibility |
| Person Responsible for Flight Operations | Day-to-day ops sign-off |
| Person Responsible for Aircraft | Airworthiness and maintenance oversight |
| Aviation Safety Manager | Safety management system |
| Quality Manager | Compliance and audit readiness |
| Security & Safety Manager | Operational security |
| RPAS Maintenance Technician | Aircraft maintenance |

> Blackfire already has the security management structure. Reframe existing staff into these post holders where the dual role is defensible. Get legal sign-off.

**Operations Manual:** Must be developed, submitted, and approved by SACAA. This is the single biggest bottleneck in the process. Cost for professional manual development: R20,000–R40,000. DC Geomatics and NTSU Aviation both offer manual development services.

**Timeline:** 12 months typical. Submit early. Budget for delays.  
**Cost:** R100,000+ all-in (courses, legal, manual, SACAA fees, post-holder training)

**Smart shortcut:** Apply for ROC while operating under DC Geomatics' UASOC in Phase 1. You generate revenue during the 12-month wait instead of pausing operations.

---

### Step 5 — PSIRA Registration for Drone Security Services (Parallel)

Drone surveillance used as a private security service in South Africa requires PSIRA (Private Security Industry Regulatory Authority) compliance in addition to SACAA certification.

**Ensure:**
- BlackFire's existing PSIRA registration covers drone-based surveillance
- Operators and pilots with guard functions are PSIRA-registered
- Scope of work in AECI contract aligns with PSIRA-defined security activities

> Cross-check with your compliance attorney. PSIRA and SACAA requirements can create an overlap that trips up operators who treated them as separate tracks.

---

### Step 6 — Insurance (Before First Flight)

Drone insurance is mandatory for commercial operations in South Africa.

**What you need:**
- Third-party liability cover
- Hull cover (crash, theft, water damage)
- Operator: Hollard is the primary specialist insurer for SACAA-licensed operators in SA

**Requirement:** RPL/RPC is mandatory to insure against crash and third-party liability  
**Theft cover:** Available without RPL for ground-only theft/damage

## PART THREE: GETTING OPERATIONAL ON SITE

---

### Step 11 — Site Survey (Before Any Hardware Arrives)

Before the drone flies at AECI, someone with authority needs to walk the site and answer these questions:

**Airspace assessment:**
- Is AECI within 10km of an airport? (If so, SACAA airspace approval needed before any flight)
- Are there restricted or controlled airspace overlays above the site?
- What is the highest structure on site that poses a flight hazard?

**Site infrastructure:**
- Where will the Dock 3 be mounted? (Requires ground fixing, power, and internet)
- What is the power supply specification at the proposed dock location?
- What cellular signal strength exists across the patrol perimeter? (4G dead zones kill autonomous operations)
- Where are the blind spots that current fixed cameras miss? (This becomes your route-planning input)

**Operational constraints:**
- Are there active operations (vehicle movements, crane activity) that restrict drone flight zones?
- Are there hazmat zones that require flight exclusion or special approach protocols?
- Who is the on-site authorised contact for flight authorisation?

**Output:** A site survey report that becomes the foundation of your Operations Manual schedule for AECI. This document protects BlackFire legally if an incident occurs.

---

### Step 12 — Mission Planning (Week Before Go-Live)

Using DJI FlightHub 2, plan the following mission types before the client sees a single flight:

**Routine Patrol Mission**
- Waypoint route covering entire perimeter
- Altitude set to clear all site structures
- Route timing set to complete a full perimeter in under 12 minutes
- Randomise departure intervals so the pattern is not predictable

**Alarm Response Mission**
- Pre-defined "Fly-To" waypoints at each gate, access point, and sensor zone
- Response drone airborne in under 20 seconds from trigger
- Live stream routed to AECI control room and BlackFire operations centre simultaneously

**Hot Spot Inspection Mission**
- Thermal-focused routes over storage tanks, electrical infrastructure, and pipeline runs
- Schedule: twice per 24-hour period (midday peak heat, 02:00 baseline)
- Anomaly detection threshold configured to AECI's safety parameters

> Have all three mission types ready to demonstrate before the first client walkthrough. The demo is not an introduction — it is a confirmation of competence.

---

### Step 13 — Onsite Training (2–3 Days)

Who needs training and on what:

**AECI Control Room Operators**
- Receiving and interpreting live drone feeds in their VMS (Video Management System)
- Recognising alarm-triggered flight notifications
- Basic incident response protocol when drone footage confirms a threat
- What the drone **cannot** do (manage expectations clearly)

**BlackFire Guards On Site**
- Radio protocol for drone-assisted response (drone eyes guide foot response)
- How to interpret thermal imagery relayed from the control room
- What to do if the drone experiences a technical failure mid-patrol

**BlackFire Drone Operators**
- FlightHub 2 mission management
- Manual override procedures
- Emergency landing protocols
- Battery and dock maintenance schedule
- SACAA incident reporting obligations

**Duration:** 2 days classroom + 1 day live flight exercise  
**Training materials:** BlackFire to develop site-specific SOPs using GoUAV or DC Geomatics templates as a base

---

### Step 14 — Integration with AECI's Existing Systems

This is where the solution goes from impressive to indispensable.

**Video Management System (VMS):** DJI FlightHub 2 streams video into Genetec or Milestone (both common in industrial environments). Confirm AECI's VMS and test the integration before go-live. If they use a different platform, confirm FlightHub 2 API compatibility.

**Alarm/Access Control Integration:** Configure the Dock 3 to receive triggers from AECI's intrusion detection system (IDS) or access control system. A door breach at the eastern perimeter automatically dispatches the drone to that sector.

**Incident Reporting:** Post-mission media (video, thermal stills) auto-uploads to FlightHub 2 cloud storage. AECI receives timestamped evidence packages for every incident — usable for insurance claims, SAPS reporting, and internal investigations.

## PART FOUR: THE DEMO

---

### Step 15 — Structure the Demo for Maximum Impact

The demo is not a technology show. It is a business case you fly in front of them.

**Who to invite:**
- AECI Operations Manager (approves it operationally)
- AECI Security Manager (becomes your internal champion)
- AECI CFO or Finance representative (sign-off authority)
- If possible: AECI's insurer or risk consultant (drone footage reduces premiums)

**Running order:**

**Part 1 — The Problem (10 minutes, boardroom)**  
Walk them through their own site's blind spots using satellite imagery. Do not lecture them — ask questions. "How long does it take your team to respond when Gate 3 triggers?" Let the problem frame itself.

**Part 2 — The Live Demonstration (20 minutes, site)**  
- Launch autonomous patrol from dock. No operator touching anything. Walk with them while it flies itself.
- Trigger a simulated alarm. Show 20-second airborne response.
- Switch to thermal view. Point the camera at a warm vehicle engine or running equipment. Show them what a heat anomaly looks like on screen.
- Activate the speaker. Let them hear the deterrence message from 50m up.

**Part 3 — The Numbers (15 minutes, boardroom)**  
- Current cost of guards covering the same perimeter
- Incidents missed (false alarms chased, real events responded to slowly)
- Proposed BlackFire drone-integrated service cost
- ROI: cost per verified incident vs current model
- Insurance premium reduction potential (ask their broker to confirm in writing before the meeting)

**Part 4 — The Close (10 minutes)**  
A 90-day pilot agreement. Low commitment. Clear success metrics agreed upfront. Makes "yes" easy.

### Step 17 — Build the Revenue Model

Drone services can be structured in three ways. Each suits a different client relationship.

**Model A — Drone-as-a-Service (DaaS) Monthly Retainer**  
BlackFire operates the drone as part of the integrated security service. Client pays a single monthly fee covering guards + drone. BlackFire owns the hardware and recovers cost through the retainer over 18–24 months.

*Best for:* Long-term contracts (12+ months), clients who don't want to own technology

| Item | Monthly Cost (est.) |
|---|---|
| Hardware amortisation (Dock 3 + 4TD over 36 months) | ~R12,000–R15,000 |
| Operator labour (1 dedicated operator, part-time) | ~R8,000–R12,000 |
| Software, SIM, maintenance | ~R3,500–R5,000 |
| **Minimum breakeven charge to client** | **~R23,500–R32,000/month** |
| **Recommended retainer rate** | **R40,000–R60,000/month** |

---

**Model B — Drone-as-Equipment (Client-Owned, BlackFire-Operated)**  
Client purchases the hardware (or finances it). BlackFire charges a monthly operations and management fee. Client retains the asset on their balance sheet.

*Best for:* Larger clients with capex budgets, clients where asset ownership aids board approval

| Item | Monthly Cost (est.) |
|---|---|
| Operations management fee | R18,000–R25,000/month |
| Maintenance and consumables | R5,000–R8,000/month |
| Software licensing (passed through) | ~R2,000–R3,000/month |
| **Total monthly charge** | **~R25,000–R36,000/month** |

---

**Model C — Call-Out / Incident Response**  
Drone deployed on demand for high-risk events, perimeter breaches, or scheduled inspections. Billed per deployment.

*Best for:* Trial clients, clients with lower security frequency needs, add-on to existing contracts

| Deployment Type | Rate |
|---|---|
| Standard patrol deployment (< 2 hours) | R3,500–R5,500 |
| Night operation deployment | R5,000–R8,000 |
| Thermal inspection (infrastructure) | R6,000–R10,000 |
| Emergency response call-out | R7,500–R12,000 |

### Step 18 — The Board Approval Toolkit for Clients

Your client's security manager wants the drone. Their CFO needs to justify it. Their board needs to approve it. You need to give the security manager the tools to walk into that boardroom and win.

**The Cost of Not Having It**  
Build a simple model for AECI:

- Annual guard cost for perimeter coverage (headcount × salary × hours)
- Number of incidents in the past 12 months that went undetected or had delayed response
- Estimated financial loss per incident (theft, damage, regulatory fine, insurance excess)
- Insurance premium for current security profile vs potential reduction with verified coverage

**The Risk Argument**  
A chemical plant has legal obligations under the Occupational Health and Safety Act and environmental legislation. An undetected perimeter breach at a hazmat site creates liability that extends to the board personally. A drone patrol provides an auditable record of security diligence. This is not about technology — it is about governance.

**The Benchmark Argument**  
Globally, 94% of corporate security incidents involve a blind spot that was known but unmonitored. Show AECI's board three publicly reported industrial security failures at comparable facilities and what they cost. Then show them what BlackFire's drone-integrated solution would have changed.

**The Competitor Pressure Argument**  
AECI's peer companies in the industrial and chemical sector are evaluating or deploying drone security now. Being the first at their site is a governance advantage, not a risk.

**The Pilot Programme Argument**  
Frame the first 90 days as a risk-free evaluation with defined success metrics agreed in advance:
- Response time to simulated alarm events (target: under 60 seconds)
- Perimeter patrol completion rate (target: 100% of scheduled patrols completed)
- False alarm reduction rate (target: 40% reduction in guard call-outs)
- Thermal anomaly detection events (number and classification)

At 90 days, the data speaks. The board votes on results, not on promises.

## PART SEVEN: RISKS AND HOW TO MANAGE THEM

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| SACAA delays ROC beyond 12 months | Operate under DC Geomatics UASOC in the interim; do not pause revenue |
| Drone hardware failure mid-contract | DJI Care Enterprise Plus covers most failure modes; maintain spare battery sets |
| Client cancels during pilot phase | Cap hardware commitment to Matrice 4T until contract is confirmed at 12+ months |
| Cellular dead zones on AECI site | Test 4G coverage during site survey; install 4G repeater if needed |
| Pilot leaves or loses licence | Train two pilots minimum; licence continuity clause in employment contracts |
| PSIRA scope challenge | Legal opinion confirming BlackFire's existing registration covers drone surveillance |
| Weather grounding (wind > 12 m/s) | Document in SLA as an excluded operational condition; build contingency patrol protocols |
| Data privacy concerns from AECI personnel | POPIA-compliant data handling policy in contract; footage access restricted to named roles |
