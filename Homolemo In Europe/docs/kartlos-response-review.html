Bottom line, updated 12 Jul 2026

The family's own read on the club-change question has settled this, and it's a genuinely good piece of reasoning: FC Orbi is currently in a promotion fight, which means real pressure on the coach to field proven players rather than give minutes to an unproven 18-year-old newcomer. A club without that pressure is actually a better fit for what the family is optimizing for — development and game time, not a name on the shirt. Not being pinned to one specific club is now seen as a feature, in service of that same priority, not a red flag to resolve before moving forward. See "Resolved since this review" below for what that changes and what still stands.

Section 1 — The club changed

FC Orbi is gone from the paperwork

Every hour of due diligence so far — the squad numbers, the foreign-player quota, the Khashuri-not-Tbilisi correction, the B-team risk — was built around FC Orbi specifically. The Payment Agreement no longer mentions FC Orbi anywhere. Clause 4 now reads:

"Club Placement" clause, as written

Critical

"The Parents and Representative acknowledge and agree that the Player will be signed with one of the following clubs, depending on the final agreement and registration process: FC Iveria; FC Iberia 2010."

Signing this authorizes a placement with either club, before knowing which — none of the FC Orbi-specific research transfers automatically to either one.

Kartlos's letter explains the switch this way: "The change from the previously discussed club option has been made after further evaluation in order to provide the Player with a stronger environment for development, better exposure, and a clearer pathway for progression." Checked independently, that claim only holds for one of the two options.

FC Iveria

Khashuri — Liga 4 (Georgia's 4th tier)
  • Plays at Grigol Jomartidze Stadium, Khashuri — the exact same ground FC Orbi uses
  • Promoted to Liga 4 after winning Regional League B in 2024
  • Most successful period was the early 1990s, in the old top-tier Umaglesi Liga, before relegation in 1997; has sat in lower divisions since
This is one division below FC Orbi's Liga 3, at the same stadium in the same town — the opposite of "a stronger environment," at least on paper.

FC Iberia 2010

Tbilisi — Liga 3 (Georgia's 3rd tier)
  • Founded 2010; just won Liga 4 in 2025 and earned first-ever promotion to Liga 3, with games still to play when it was secured
  • Based in Tbilisi, matching the original cost-of-living/safety research (unlike FC Orbi, which turned out to be in Khashuri)
  • Same tier as FC Orbi, but no prior history at that level to judge against
Same division as FC Orbi, but an unproven club there — not yet independently verified to the depth FC Orbi was.
Original concern, now resolved deliberately rather than left open: this section's finding stands as fact — Iveria is a tier below, Iberia 2010 is unproven at Liga 3 — but the family's own reasoning below changes how much that matters.
Resolved Since This Review

Why staying flexible on the club is actually the right call

FC Orbi is currently fighting for promotion. A club in that position has real pressure on the coach to put out his strongest, most proven side every week — not to blood an unproven 18-year-old still finding his feet. That's exactly the environment least likely to deliver what the family actually wants: real minutes, not a bench role at a "better" club on paper. A club without promotion pressure has less incentive to protect results at the expense of giving a newcomer a genuine look.

On that basis, the family is comfortable proceeding without pinning down FC Iveria vs. FC Iberia 2010 in advance — the point isn't which specific badge he wears, it's which environment actually gets him playing. The tier/quality gap flagged above is still true and still worth knowing, but it's no longer being treated as a blocker.
