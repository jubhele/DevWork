Conversation Guide

Talking to Geoff about the handover to full-time European representation

Re
Shange Homolemo Amunene ("Amu") — handover conversation with Geoffrey Cockrill
Purpose
Work out, together with Geoff, whether the prospective agent (Kartlos Rukhaia / Sports Consulting Management) will steward Amu's development — not just place him and move on
Family position
Continuing the opportunity, not exiting it. These are the questions a careful parent asks before signing over stewardship of an 18-year-old's career, not an attempt to talk Geoff out of the deal
Timing
Deposit decision by 13 Jul 2026 — have this conversation before then
Why This Conversation

Geoff got Amu to this point. That counts for a lot, and it's worth saying to him directly before anything else.

What's changing now isn't the relationship with Geoff — it's the size of the decision. Up to now Amu has been developing under people the family knows personally. The next step hands his day-to-day football life to someone in another country the family has never met. That's a bigger step than any before it, and it deserves the same care Geoff himself has clearly put into getting Amu here.

The family has already committed real money and time to this opportunity and has no interest in walking away from it. But there's a difference between an agent who gets Amu signed and an agent who gets Amu developed — more game time, more experience, a real pathway — and at 18, that difference matters more than almost anything else in his career. This conversation is how the family finds out, with Geoff's help, which one Kartlos actually is.

The Question Underneath All the Others
Litmus Test

Is Amu being signed to play, or signed to be on a roster?

Every question below is really this one question asked from a different angle. Keep it in mind through the whole conversation — it's the fastest way to tell whether an answer is reassuring or just smooth.

Agent measures success in minutes played, levels climbed, and moves made when a placement stalls
vs
Agent measures success in signatures, fees, and roster size
Opening It Well
Suggested opening, in your own words

"Geoff, before we go further with Kartlos, I want to sit down with you properly. You've gotten Amu this far and I trust what you've built with him — this isn't me second-guessing that. But handing him over to someone in Georgia is a bigger step than anything so far, and I want to go into it with my eyes open, the same way I'd want to know if it were my own son."

"I'm not trying to find a reason to pull out. We've already put real money and time behind this and I want it to work. I just need us — you, me and Mpho — to be straight with each other about whether this agent is going to fight for Amu's development, or whether we're just the newest name on his books."

"And one more thing before we get into it — you've earned to be paid properly for everything you've put into getting Amu here. I don't want that sitting as some informal arrangement on the side. Let's agree a fair number and put it in the deal in writing, so it's done right by you too."

Question Themes

Six areas to cover. They don't need to come out in this order — let the conversation breathe — but try to touch all six before you leave.

1. Development track record

Philosophy
The single best predictor of what Kartlos will do for Amu is what he's actually done for players like him before.
  • In the last five years, how many players has Kartlos taken from Amu's age and level all the way to regular first-team football? Can we get names and clubs, not just numbers?
  • What happened to the ones who didn't make it — did the agency stay involved and help them find another club, or did they drop off the books?
  • If Amu isn't playing regularly three months in, is there an actual next step already in Kartlos's head, or is it "let's see how it goes"?

2. Who gets paid, and for what

Incentives
An agent paid in full on signature is financially done the day Amu joins the club — whether he plays or not. An agent whose money is tied to Amu playing has a reason to keep working after the ink dries.
  • Is any part of Kartlos's €13,500 fee tied to Amu actually getting on the pitch, or is it earned in full the moment he signs?
  • If Amu has to move clubs later because this one isn't working, does Kartlos get paid again for the new move? Could that make him slower to admit the first placement isn't working?

3. Formalizing Geoff's own pay

Fair Pay
This one isn't a suspicion — it's a principle. Geoff developed Amu to this point and brought this opportunity to the family; he's earned to be paid for that. The point of asking isn't to find out whether he's being compensated, it's to make sure he is, properly and in writing, as a formal part of this deal rather than an informal side arrangement nobody agreed to on paper.
  • What does Geoff think is fair compensation for the work that got Amu here and for brokering the introduction to Kartlos — and let's agree a figure together.
  • Can that be written into the deal as its own explicit, transparent line, rather than left as an undisclosed referral arrangement with Sports Consulting Management?
  • Is Geoff staying involved as a mentor Amu can still call after the handover, or is this his last active role — and does the number reflect whichever one it is?

4. The escape valve

Priority #1
This is the family's stated priority: game time over a rushed contract. The real test is what the agent does when a placement isn't delivering that — not what he promises when it is.
  • If Amu is stuck on the bench for two or three months, what does Kartlos actually do — go looking for a better fit, or leave him there?
  • Can Kartlos point to a specific player he moved out of a bad situation into a better one — not just placed once and left?
  • Is there anything in the contract that penalizes Amu or the family for requesting a move if the placement isn't working?

5. Life outside the pitch

Welfare
Amu is 18 and will be alone in a country the family has never visited. Football is only part of what has to go right.
  • Who arranges and pays for housing and food, and who actually checks in on him in person — not just by message?
  • What's the plan if he's unwell, injured, or in crisis — who does the family call, and is that a name and number, or just "the agency"?
  • Does he get updates on studying or a fallback path if football doesn't work out, or is football the only plan?

6. Verification, openly

Transparency
A development-minded agent has nothing to lose from the family checking his record independently. Resistance here is itself an answer.
  • How well does Geoff actually know Kartlos — a long working relationship, or a first-time introduction? How did they connect?
  • Is Geoff comfortable with the family looking up Kartlos's FIFA agent license directly and speaking to two players he's previously placed abroad, independent of Geoff or Kartlos?
  • Is Kartlos open to a milestone-based payment structure — full fee, paid out as he delivers registration, squad inclusion, and real minutes — rather than everything up front? (Given the sudden timeline, the live version we're actually proposing is simpler: half at end of July, half at end of August, with nothing due before either date — see the letter for the reasoning.)
  • That milestone structure only works if Amu holds up his own side too — training attendance, fitness, discipline. Would Geoff help hold him to that from here, even after the handover, so it's clear whether any missed gate is on Kartlos, the club, or Amu himself? Before You Sign
Non-negotiable
  • Written confirmation that Kartlos's fee isn't earned in full on signature alone — some portion tied to registration, squad inclusion, and playing time
  • Geoff's own compensation agreed as a fair, explicit figure and written into the deal — not an undisclosed side arrangement
  • A named, reachable human in Georgia the family can contact directly — not only Kartlos, not only "the club"
  • A stated plan for what happens if Amu isn't playing meaningful minutes within an agreed window
  • Family free to verify Kartlos's FIFA license and speak to his two prior foreign placements without resistance
Nice to have
  • Regular scheduled video check-ins with the family, not only ad hoc updates
  • Introduction to other South African or English-speaking players already at the club
  • Support continuing some form of study alongside football
Leaving the Conversation

Close by asking Geoff for three things in writing before the deposit decision: Kartlos's track record with names and clubs, an agreed figure for Geoff's own compensation built into the written deal, and a response to the milestone payment structure (the full term sheet is in the companion due diligence memo, linked at the top of this page).

End it the way you opened it — with thanks, not suspicion. The goal isn't to catch Geoff out. It's to make sure the next person holding Amu's career cares about the same thing Geoff has clearly cared about so far: not just getting him signed, but getting him better.
