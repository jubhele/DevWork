Our honest bottom line

Both FC Orbi and Sports Consulting Management check out as real — that already puts this ahead of the outright scams we read about while looking into this. But the fee structure, the way all the financial risk currently sits on Amu's side, and the size of the fee relative to the wage on offer match a pattern player unions and journalists have kept documenting right through 2025, across the same landscape South African, Ghanaian and Nigerian families keep losing money in. We want to be clear: none of that means this is a scam. It means it's exactly the kind of deal that deserves real verification before more money moves — which is what the rest of this is.

What we found reading the contract itself

All the monetary risk in the agreement as written currently runs one direction, so this is the part we'd most love your read on:

The deposit and second payment are non-refundable no matter what happens. If the 31 July payment is even a day late, the representation agreement ends immediately and everything paid so far — including the deposit — stays with the agency, no matter the reason. That's the single biggest thing we'd want changed before anything more moves, and it's the main thinking behind the two-payment structure we proposed above.

A few more things worth putting plainly:

  • The economics don't clear for years: €13,500 total against wages of about €170/month. Over the full possible 12 months, that's roughly €2,040 in wages against a €13,500 fee — not a percentage of a real transfer or salary, but a flat charge that dwarfs anything Amu actually earns from the arrangement itself.
  • If the September payment is late, the current wording doesn't cancel the deal either — it lets the club push him to the reserve/B team indefinitely and "suspend" representation, with no refund and no clean exit. We'd like a real, mutual exit clause instead.
  • We're planning to cover Amu's accommodation ourselves regardless of how the fee conversation lands — it doesn't change what's owed to Kartlos on paper, but it means we, not the club or agency, control where he actually lives.
  • A player covering his own agent's fee is allowed under FIFA's rules, so this isn't unusual on that front by itself. What we'd love an honest answer on is the size of it: how much of the €13,500 is Kartlos's own commission, versus costs passed through on the club's behalf?
  • Being "listed on Transfermarkt" gets mentioned as a selling point in places, but those listings can be added by any volunteer editor for almost any registered lower-league player — worth knowing it doesn't really confirm anything about career prospects on its own.

One thing we want to confirm directly rather than assume: FC Orbi's home ground is actually listed in Khashuri, about 90–100km from Tbilisi — not the capital, which is what a lot of our other research below assumed. Before anything else moves forward, we'd genuinely love to know plainly: will he actually be living and training in Khashuri, Tbilisi, or somewhere else, day to day?

And on the "B team now, first team later" framing: Georgian reserve teams are structurally required to play in a lower division than the first team, so if that's the real plan, it likely means Liga 4 or the Regionuli Liga rather than a depth squad in the same league Amu would already be starting in. Since showing him off is such a big part of why we're doing this at all, and that level has essentially no video coverage, we'd love clarity on two things: does FC Orbi even have a registered B team, and if the plan changes, which division would he actually be playing in?

For reference, the payment schedule as currently written: €4,000 deposit before 13 Jul 2026; €4,750 by 31 Jul 2026 (agreement terminates and the deposit is forfeited if late); €4,750 by 30 Sep 2026 (reserve/B-team demotion if late). This is what our two-payment proposal above is meant to replace.

What we'd like to verify before the next payment

All of this costs nothing and takes days, not weeks:

  • Look up Kartlos on FIFA's own agent register (agents.fifa.com) rather than take the agency's word on license status
  • Contact FC Orbi directly through the Georgian Football Federation, not through Kartlos or yourself, and confirm they know of this signing independently

None of this means we think it's necessarily a bad deal. FC Orbi and the agency both check out as real, which is more than most of the cautionary stories out there can say. It's just that "real entities" and "fair deal" are two different questions, and the second one is worth being thorough about.

Other routes and people we looked into

We want to be upfront that we looked well beyond just this one offer — not because we're set on going elsewhere, but because we wanted to actually know what else exists before saying yes to this one.

Going direct, with no agent at all: a player is always free to approach and negotiate with a club directly under FIFA's own rules — an agent is a service you can buy, not a requirement. Amu already has the biggest advantage that playbook depends on: he's physically in Georgia, training, and visible. A tight highlight reel, a one-page CV, and direct outreach to coaching staff at other Liga 3/Liga 2 clubs costs nothing and can run in parallel with FC Orbi.

Two more established Tbilisi agencies turned up, for context on what a bigger book looks like: FA Players Agents (Republic Square, Tbilisi, +995 577 311777, info@playersagents.ge — 23 players, 12 in the Georgian top flight, €19.18m combined value) and Free Football (Iv. Kereselidze 1, Tbilisi, +995 598 444888, info@freefootball.ge — 27 players, 15 top-flight, €32.13m). Both a different scale to SCM entirely, though it's not established either takes on completely unattached trialists the way SCM does — worth a direct call to ask.

SCM's own two foreign placements are honestly the closest real reference point we have for what Kartlos actually delivers: Blessing Assamoah (Ghana), now at Erovnuli Liga 2 — one level above where Amu would start — and Francis Anyanwu (Nigeria), who reached FC Telavi in the actual top flight before it was relegated in 2025, and is currently without a club. We'd love to reach either of them, ideally through their clubs directly or through GEFA (the Georgian Footballers' Association, a FIFPRO-affiliated players' union) rather than through Kartlos or yourself.

FIFA-licensed South African agents are also worth a call, since their fees are capped and tied to an actual signed transaction rather than a large deposit paid up front: Ryan Hartslief at Bigtime Sports Academy (FIFA licence via SAFA), Karabo Mathang-Tshabuse at P Management, and Mike Makaab at ProSport International in Durban. These agents typically work with players already inside academy structures, so fit isn't guaranteed, but a genuine licensed agent should take an introductory call without asking for a non-refundable deposit first.

A no-fee alternative worth knowing about: former Bafana Bafana defender Mark Fish's foundation has a standing partnership with English Championship club Charlton Athletic to assess South African youth players at the club's own academy — the club evaluates for free, the foundation funds logistics, and there's no forfeiture clause because no money changes hands up front.

And on the pricier end, Spain Rush-SPF in Valencia is a genuine, longer-running version of the "training and exposure, no guarantee" model — residential academy since 2016, 500+ alumni, South Africa specifically named as a source country, real named partner clubs. Trials run 2 weeks to 3 months with no guaranteed contract sold. Pricing is roughly €35,000 though, well over double this deal — not a cheaper alternative, a different and much larger commitment.
