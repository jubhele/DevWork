Both the club and the agency are real, which puts this a step above the outright fabricated schemes documented below. But the fee structure, the all-risk-on-player forfeiture clauses, and the size of the ask relative to the wage on offer match the profile player unions and investigative reporters were still documenting as recently as 2025, across the same fake-agent economy South African, Ghanaian and Nigerian families keep losing money to1. Section 2 — Reading the contract itself

Where the risk actually sits in the clauses

All monetary risk in this agreement runs one direction. Below are the clauses that matter most, in plain terms.

Deposit and payment 2 are non-refundable no matter what happens

Critical

If the 31 July payment is even a day late, the representation agreement terminates immediately, the player has no right to continue the placement, and every euro already paid — including the €4,000 deposit — is explicitly kept by the agency with "no obligation to refund."

"All amounts previously paid to the Agency... shall be considered non-refundable. The Agency shall have no obligation to refund any money already paid."

The economics don't clear for years

Critical

Total cost is €13,500. The wage on offer is 500 GEL/month — roughly €170. Over the full possible 12-month contract (6 months + a discretionary 6-month extension the club doesn't have to grant), that's about €2,040 in wages against a €13,500 outlay. The fee is not a percentage of a real transfer or salary — it's a flat placement charge that dwarfs anything the player earns from the arrangement itself.

Self-funding accommodation is worth doing regardless of the fee outcome

Good instinct

The club's own in-kind promise (private apartment, Section "Football contract terms") is separate from the €13,500 agency fee — offering to cover accommodation and living costs directly doesn't reduce what's owed to Kartlos on paper. But it's still worth doing, for a reason unrelated to money: it puts the family, not the club or agency, in control of where Amunene actually lives. That directly addresses the exact failure mode in the fake-agent pattern in Section 3 — promised housing that turns out substandard or simply doesn't materialize. Worth raising in the same conversation as Section 12's milestone proposal: if the family is covering accommodation independently, ask Kartlos directly what the €13,500 is now paying for, since one of the in-kind benefits it was implicitly bundled with is no longer needed from his side.

Missing payment 3 doesn't cancel the deal — it demotes the player

High

If the September payment is late, the agency doesn't have to refund anything or release the player either. Instead it can register him to the reserve/B team indefinitely and "suspend or limit further representation services" — keeping leverage without an exit for the family.

The player is funding his own placement, not the club

Context

FIFA's 2023 agent regulations do allow a "client-pays" model where a player covers their own agent's fee6. That makes this technically permissible. But the standard guidance for spotting fake-agent schemes is specifically that legitimate scouts and clubs do not require the player to fund the trial or placement itself, and FIFA rules expect the inviting club to cover travel and accommodation for a genuine trial5. A 3rd-tier club paying €170/month has no obvious commercial reason to need a €13,500 intermediary fee to sign a trialist it already trained for free.

Geoffrey Cockrill signs the agency's contract as the player's "Representative"

Context

The same person relaying encouraging updates to the family in the WhatsApp thread ("well done to Amu thus far") is named in the agreement as the Player's signing representative to the Georgian agency — not as an independent adviser to the family. Worth asking directly, in writing: does Cockrill or the South African coaching setup receive a referral fee, commission, or any payment from Sports Consulting Management for this placement? That's a standard conflict-of-interest question, not an accusation — and the family's position going in is that Cockrill has earned to be paid for the work that got the player this far. The point of asking isn't to uncover a hidden payment, it's to agree a fair figure and put it in writing as its own explicit line in the deal, rather than leave it as an undisclosed side arrangement.

"Listed on Transfermarkt" is a low-value selling point

Context

Transfermarkt player pages can be requested by anyone and added by volunteer editors for essentially any registered lower-league player — it costs nothing and confirms nothing about career prospects. Framing it as a contract deliverable is a soft persuasion tactic, not a meaningful guarantee.

FC Orbi's home ground checks out to Khashuri, not Tbilisi

High — verify directly

The club's own contact listing places its stadium (Grigol Jomartidze, 2,000 seats) at "Sport Strasse, Khashuri, Georgia"3 — a town of about 28,000 people roughly 90–100km (~1.5–2 hrs) west of Tbilisi29, not the capital. Some aggregator pages label the club "Tbilisi," likely an office or admin address rather than where matches are actually played. This isn't a red flag by itself, but it does mean the cost-of-living and safety research in Section 8 — and the Tbilisi-as-a-base framing in Section 13 — was built around the wrong city if he'll actually be living and training in or near Khashuri day to day. Ask directly: where will he actually live and train — Khashuri, Tbilisi, or somewhere else — before signing anything further.

"B team now, first team later" may mean a different, lower league entirely

High

In the Georgian league system, reserve/B teams don't just sit lower in the same squad — they're structurally required to play in a lower division than their parent club, and can never be promoted past it30. FC Orbi's first team is in Liga 3; if it fields a registered "B team," that side would be playing in Liga 4 or Regionuli Liga — the 4th or 5th tier, not a depth-squad version of the same league. This research pass found no public evidence FC Orbi even has a registered reserve side, which cuts both ways: it needs direct confirmation either way. If it does exist, video coverage of Liga 4/Regionuli Liga football is essentially nonexistent on the platforms that cover Liga 331 — which matters enormously given the family's priority is showcasing him, not just getting him minutes nobody sees.

Two direct questions worth putting to Kartlos and the club before anything else: (1) does FC Orbi have a registered B team, and if so, which division does it actually play in; and (2) is the "first team with B-team steppingstone" framing describing a real, near-term pathway, or is it the same B-team demotion the contract already threatens as a penalty for a late third payment (Section 2, above) — just introduced earlier and reframed as the starting plan.

Section 6 — Alternative routes worth a phone call

Other agencies and pathways to approach

There isn't a direct like-for-like competitor selling "guaranteed Georgian third-tier placement for a flat fee" — see Section 3 on why that offering itself is unusual. The realistic alternative isn't another version of this deal, it's going through the normal, regulated South African agent channel instead of a Tbilisi-based agency the family has no independent way to check on.

Does he even need an agent right now?

Worth trying first — free

Under FIFA's own regulations, a player is always free to approach and negotiate with a club directly — an agent is a service he can buy, not a requirement to get signed26. That matters a lot here, because the thing SCM is actually selling is introduction — getting a club to look at him — and a strong highlight reel plus direct outreach does the same job for free.

The practical version of this, used successfully at non-league/lower-division level generally27:

  • A tight 2–3 minute highlight reel (best actions first) hosted on YouTube, plus a one-page player CV
  • Direct outreach by email and Instagram DM to club coaching staff — lower-division clubs are often genuinely reachable this way, more so than top-flight clubs
  • Offer to train first rather than asking for a trial outright — an easier yes for a coach to give
  • Follow up once after 5–7 days if there's no response, then move to the next club

He already has the single biggest advantage this playbook depends on: he's physically in Georgia, training, and visible. That lets the family test direct outreach to other Liga 3 and Liga 2 clubs in parallel with anything happening at FC Orbi — at zero cost, and without touching the exclusivity question in Section 11's term sheet until something concrete comes back.

Section 7 — Foreign player policy

What actually governs a foreign signing in Georgian football

No public, official squad-quota number (e.g. "max N foreign players") for Liga 3 turned up in this research pass — the GFF's regulatory documents aren't indexed publicly at that level of detail, so treat the "only one quota spot left" pitch as unverified rather than false.

Older reporting on a since-closed Tbilisi club (2017 — outside the 5-year window this research now applies, so not relied on here as a current fact) described Georgian clubs paying the GFF a registration fee as high as 15,000 lari (~€8,000) to place a single foreign player on the roster. This pass could not confirm a current 2026 figure from GFF's own published documents. Rather than treat that old number as fact, use it as the reason to ask a direct question:

  • Is FC Orbi actually registering Amunene with the GFF as a professional foreign player — or would he train and play informally, unregistered? That's the mechanism behind most of the fake-agent cases in Section 3.
  • Ask the agency for an itemized breakdown of the €13,500: how much is the agency's own commission versus costs passed through on the club's behalf (registration, accommodation, admin)?
  • Ask GFF or the club directly, in writing, for Liga 3's actual foreign-player squad limit — FC Orbi's own roster already carries 3 foreign players out of 20 (15%), which should factor into how much urgency the "last spot" framing deserves3.

Reluctance to accept a structure like this is itself informative. Pair this with Section 14's independent monthly check-ins so the family isn't relying on the agency's word to confirm each gate has actually been hit.

Since the actual goal is showcasing him, not just playing him: add one more non-negotiable to Milestones 2 and 3 — footage. Georgian Liga 3 has thin video coverage and Liga 4/Regionuli Liga (Section 2's B-team finding) has essentially none, so playing time that nobody records doesn't accomplish what the family actually wants. Don't rely on the club or league to provide this: have someone film every match from the stands on a phone, tripod is enough. That footage is the actual deliverable — it's what turns into the highlight reel for Section 3's direct-outreach option regardless of how the FC Orbi situation plays out.

Making it two-sided: what Amu commits to at each gate

A milestone schedule that only tests Kartlos and asks nothing of Amu isn't fair to Kartlos, and it wastes the moment. Amu is 18 — this placement is also where "professional" starts meaning something in his own daily habits, not just his talent. Pairing the agent's payment gates with the player's own commitments makes it obvious, gate by gate, whether a miss is on Kartlos, on the club, or on Amu himself.

GateWhat Amu commits toWho verifies it
0. Trial Full effort and attendance at every trial session; follows coaching instructions; no unexplained absences Family/Geoff cross-check directly with the coaching staff, not the agency
1. GFF registration Completes his own medical, paperwork, and eligibility steps on time; stays in full training condition while it's processed Confirm with the club that nothing was held up on the player's side

If Amu holds up every one of his own commitments — full attendance, full fitness, clean conduct — and the minutes still don't come, that's strong, specific evidence the placement itself is the problem, not him, and the case for invoking Section 11's exit clause gets much stronger. If instead training attendance or discipline is slipping, that's a different and more immediate conversation to have directly with Amu — ideally with Geoff involved, since he knows him best — before any blame lands on the club.
Section 13 — Georgia as a base

The actual plan: a 6+ month regional base, not a fallback

This is no longer a contingency for if FC Orbi falls through — it's the standing plan the family has already discussed directly with Geoff. Georgia becomes Amu's base for 6+ months regardless of how the Kartlos placement specifically plays out, with FC Orbi as one opportunity being pursued from that base, not a precondition for it. South African passport holders get a full year visa-free in Georgia36, so the base itself carries no visa pressure — what matters is what's actually reachable from it without needing to fly every time.

Section 14 — Staying on track

A simple checkpoint plan

Whichever deal is signed, the family should be checking the same four things on a fixed schedule — independently of what the agency reports.

Gate 0
GFF registration confirmed
Get written confirmation from the club (not the agency) that he's officially registered. No further payment before this is independently verified.
Week 2–4
First matchday squad inclusion?
Check the club's own matchday squad list or GFF/Soccerway match reports directly — not a message from Kartlos or Cockrill.
Week 6
Red-flag checkpoint
Zero competitive minutes by here, despite full fitness, is the trigger point to invoke whatever exit/renegotiation clause was won in Section 11 — or to start the Georgia-base scan in Section 12 for real.
Monthly
Written status update
Insist on a named club contact — not the agency — for a short monthly note: appearances, minutes, injuries, disciplinary status. Pair it with Amu's own account of training attendance and conditioning (Section 12's player-accountability table) so the family has both sides, not just the club's.
Ongoing — 6+ months
Georgia stays the base regardless
The Week 6 checkpoint above decides whether to keep pushing the FC Orbi placement specifically — it does not decide whether Amu stays in Georgia. That's a separate, already-made decision (Section 13): a 6+ month regional base, agreed with Geoff, independent of how any single club situation plays out. A weak Week 6 reading means pivoting to Section 13's other options from the same base, not packing up.
Keep every future payment traceable and receipted against a specific milestone in the contract — never an informal transfer against a verbal promise.
