1. The role model — and the cautionary tales

Anthony Hamilton (Lewis), Richard Williams (Serena & Venus), and Mathew Knowles (Beyoncé) share three things worth copying:

  • They documented everything early. Footage of a 8-year-old Lewis karting is part of the brand today. Every training session, match, and milestone of Amu's from now on is future brand material.
  • They protected the child from the business. The parent absorbed negotiations, media, and money conversations; the athlete's only job was performance. Amu trains and plays; you handle Kartlos, sponsors, and platforms.
  • They built the story before the fame. The narrative ("father mortgaged everything", "coached from a Compton court") existed and was told consistently long before global success validated it.

The cautionary side: Mathew Knowles was eventually fired by his own daughter; Jomo Sono-era player-management in SA has a history of blurred money lines. Build in separation early: a family agreement on what percentage/role you hold as manager, written down now while it's easy, reviewed when Amu turns of age or signs professionally. It protects the relationship more than the money.

3. Name protection & trademark (practical steps)

I can prepare everything, but trademark filing is a legal act done in your name — budget for it and consider a CIPC-registered agent for the SA filing.
  1. Lock digital assets first (this week, cheap, instant): register homolemoshange.com (+ .co.za), and claim the handle — same on every platform — e.g. @homolemoshange or @shange10. Do this before any public visibility; handles are free until someone else takes them.
  2. South Africa: file with CIPC (Companies and Intellectual Property Commission) — trademark classes to consider: Class 41 (sporting activities/entertainment), Class 25 (clothing/merch), Class 35 (advertising/endorsement services). Cost is modest (a few hundred rand per class filing fee, more with an attorney). A minor's name can be trademarked with guardian consent.
  3. Europe/Georgia: don't file everywhere yet — file EUTM (EU-wide, via EUIPO) only once there's real commercial activity in Europe; Georgia is not in the EU (national filing at Sakpatenti if ever needed). Premature multi-territory filing burns money.
  4. Image rights: when any club contract appears, image rights must be a carved-out clause — the club licenses use of his image, it never owns it. This is the single most valuable clause for a future brand; flag it now for the first professional contract.

  1. The Craft (40%) — training clips, skill work, match highlights. Always tagged with position and age.
  2. The Journey (30%) — Georgia arrival, new club, language, food, first snow: the human story sponsors buy into.
  3. The Lineage (15%) — side-by-side clips with Khumalo/Modise/Sono moves; celebrates SA football heritage and earns local media pickup.
  4. The Family (15%) — father-and-son moments, gratitude posts, community. This is the Hamilton/Williams differentiator.

  • Amu is already in Georgia — this means footage from his current programme may already exist. Ask him what video he has on his phone from the past weeks: training, matches, life. That's the back-catalogue to launch with.

6. Data & performance tracking (the "information about him" layer)

To make the brand credible to scouts and sponsors, track him like a professional from day one:

  • Match log: every appearance — date, opponent, minutes, goals/assists, position. A simple spreadsheet is enough; it becomes the media-kit stat block and the milestone evidence for the Kartlos accountability table.
  • Physical benchmarks: quarterly — height/weight, sprint times, if the club runs GPS/fitness testing ask for the exports.
  • Video index: tag the raw footage archive by skill type (assists, dribbles, set pieces) so highlight reels take hours, not weeks.
  • Platform profiles: once registered in Georgia, get him on Transfermarkt and ensure the club reports lineups correctly — scouts live on these databases.
